\begin{abstract}
How should a robot policy spend test-time compute? For large language models, scaling inference-time computation has unlocked large capability gains; for embodied policies executing long-horizon tasks, no principled answer exists. We argue the answer is \emph{spend it at milestone boundaries, on verification}---and that doing so converts the characteristic failure mode of long-horizon manipulation from a capacity problem into a systems problem. On the RoboDojo benchmark, the strongest generalist policies earn substantial partial credit on long-horizon tasks yet rarely finish them, because per-step errors compound across dozens of stages. We propose \mile{} (\textbf{MI}lestone-grounded closed-\textbf{L}oop imagination and \textbf{E}xecution), a framework that wraps any frozen chunked VLA policy with three components: (i) an automatic extractor that segments tasks into physically grounded milestones from kinematic and visual cues, without sub-task annotation; (ii) a lightweight latent world model with a jointly trained steps-to-completion value head that verifies candidate action chunks before commitment; and (iii) a safe-anchor rollback mechanism that retreats to verified milestones on deviation. Crucially, the acceptance test is calibrated by \emph{conformal prediction}: accepted segments are guaranteed to terminate within the verification radius with probability at least $1-\alpha$, a distribution-free, finite-sample guarantee. This yields an explicit lower bound on task success in which per-stage errors enter additively rather than compounding multiplicatively with horizon. Beyond inference, failed executions are converted by hindsight milestone relabeling into recovery data, enabling reward-free self-improvement across deployment rounds. On RoboDojo, \mile{} improves long-horizon \TODO{metric} from \TODO{X} to \TODO{X} (\TODO{XX}\% relative) over the DM0.5 base, transfers to $\pi_0$ unchanged, exhibits favorable compute--success scaling with the candidate budget, and adds only \TODO{X}M trainable parameters and \TODO{X}ms per decision. Code, models, and evaluation scripts will be released.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A remarkable recent finding in machine learning is that many capability gains come not from making models bigger, but from spending more compute \emph{at inference time}: letting language models reason longer before answering~\cite{testtime}. Embodied policies have no analogue of this principle. A Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy spends exactly the same computation on a routine reach as on the one grasp on which a ten-minute task depends, executes the result open-loop, and has no mechanism to anticipate consequences, detect drift, or recover. The outcome is visible in comprehensive evaluations: on RoboDojo~\cite{robodojo}, the strongest generalist policies earn substantial \emph{partial} credit on long-horizon tasks but rarely complete them end-to-end---robots can pick the cup, and they can stack the cup, but they cannot reliably \emph{pick and then stack}. Per-step deviations of behavior-cloned policies compound multiplicatively across stages until success collapses.

This paper asks: \textbf{how should an embodied policy spend test-time compute?} Uniformly scaling per-step computation is infeasible (full-horizon imagined rollouts cost seconds per step) and unnecessary (most steps are routine). Our answer is a principle: \emph{concentrate inference-time computation at a sparse set of physically meaningful decision boundaries, and spend it on verification}. Long-horizon failure is then a systems problem rather than a capacity problem, and closing the loop at the right granularity is a cheap, general, and provable way to fix it.

We instantiate this principle as \mile{} (Milestone-grounded closed-Loop imagination and Execution). Physical milestones---gripper transitions, contact onsets, kinematic turning points, discovered automatically from demonstrations---segment a task into short stages. At each stage boundary, \mile{} samples candidate action chunks from the frozen base policy, verifies them by imagined rollouts of a lightweight latent world model scored by a steps-to-completion value head, executes only candidates that pass a \emph{conformally calibrated} acceptance test, and retreats to the most recent verified milestone on deviation. Verification is where we spend compute; milestones are where we spend it; rollback is insurance for when imagination is wrong.

Two properties distinguish \mile{} from prior uses of world models in VLA systems (which predict but do not verify, verify only at isolated events, or train inside imagination). First, the acceptance test carries a \textbf{distribution-free statistical guarantee}: by split-conformal calibration, an accepted segment terminates within the verification radius of its target milestone with probability at least $1-\alpha$, for user-chosen $\alpha$ and finite calibration data. This upgrades the compounding-error analysis from a heuristic argument to an explicit success lower bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:main}). Second, \mile{} is not only an inference procedure: every rollback automatically produces hindsight-labeled \emph{recovery data}, which is periodically distilled back into the system, yielding reward-free self-improvement across deployment rounds.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{A test-time-compute framework for embodied policies.} We formalize where (milestone boundaries), how much (candidate budgets), and how safely (conformal verification + rollback) a long-horizon policy should spend inference-time computation, and show empirically that success scales with this budget.
  \item \textbf{Conformally verified execution.} A lightweight latent world model (\TODO{X}M parameters) with a jointly trained progress-value head scores candidate chunks; split-conformal calibration gives a finite-sample, distribution-free guarantee on the acceptance test, converting the compounding-error bound from multiplicative in horizon to additive over verified segments.
  \item \textbf{Safe-anchor rollback with self-improvement.} A verified-milestone stack defines heal states explicitly; hindsight relabeling converts every retreat into recovery training data, improving success across deployment rounds without rewards or human labels.
  \item \textbf{Comprehensive evaluation.} On RoboDojo, \mile{} improves long-horizon and memory performance over DM0.5 by \TODO{XX}\% relative, transfers to $\pi_0$ unchanged, and includes compute--success scaling curves, coverage-validity checks of the conformal guarantee, and a failure-taxonomy analysis.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Vision-Language-Action models.}
VLA policies extend vision-language models with action tokens or diffusion/flow-matching action decoders~\cite{rt2,openvla,pi0,univla,groot,dm05}. Despite architectural diversity, the execution protocol is shared: reactive, open-loop action chunking conditioned on current observation and instruction. Our work is orthogonal---\mile{} is a base-agnostic wrapper, and we demonstrate it on two bases (DM0.5, $\pi_0$).

\paragraph{World models as training objectives.}
UniVLA~\cite{univla} and CauVA~\cite{cauva} show that causal-dynamics modeling is a strong post-training objective for VLAs, especially for long-horizon tasks. These methods internalize dynamics into the policy but do not \emph{use} prediction at execution time; \mile{} is complementary and can sit on top of such backbones.

\paragraph{World models as planners.}
StructVLA~\cite{structvla} replaces dense rollouts with sparse structured frames, and Act2Goal~\cite{act2goal} generates intermediate visual states for goal-conditioned control; AHEAD~\cite{ahead} predicts future latents for dynamic grasping with a frozen VLA. All predict, none verify: an erroneous imagination steers execution astray. \mile{}'s milestone latents borrow StructVLA's insight (sparsity with kinematic grounding) but serve a closed-loop verification role rather than a one-shot planning role.

\paragraph{World models as simulators.}
WoVR~\cite{wovr}, RISE~\cite{rise}, and GigaBrain~\cite{gigabrain} optimize policies inside learned imagination, with WoVR's keyframe-initialized rollouts mitigating hallucination. Our value head follows GigaBrain's finding that jointly predicting future states and steps-to-completion outperforms either objective alone; unlike these methods, \mile{} performs RL-free, inference-time verification and needs no on-policy imagined training.

\paragraph{Execution-time correction and emergent retry.}
World-in-Loop~\cite{worldinloop} triggers a corrector at isolated events (e.g., pre-grasp) and rolls back to a heuristic heal state; RB-VLA~\cite{rbvla} maintains a persistent belief state. \mile{} generalizes both: verification is triggered at every milestone boundary (a full coverage of task stages), and rollback targets are defined \emph{structurally} by the verified-milestone stack rather than heuristically. A complementary line of evidence suggests that retry-and-correct behavior can \emph{emerge} from large-scale human-data pretraining~\cite{liber0}. Emergent retry, however, offers no triggering guarantee, no defined retreat target, and no error bound, and it presupposes a data scale inaccessible to most laboratories; \mile{} instead provides a lightweight ($\sim$35M parameters), provable, and base-agnostic mechanism that is orthogonal to---and composable with---such emergent abilities.

\paragraph{Long-horizon evaluation.}
RoboDojo~\cite{robodojo} provides the first unified sim-and-real protocol with a public leaderboard, five capability dimensions, and per-sub-step scoring; its finding that long-horizon \emph{scores} exceed long-horizon \emph{success rates} is direct evidence of compounding error and motivates our design. LIBERO-X~\cite{libero_x} and CALVIN~\cite{calvin} serve as supplementary benchmarks.

\paragraph{Test-time compute and conformal prediction.}
Scaling inference-time computation has emerged as a capability axis for language models~\cite{testtime}, but embodied policies lack a principled account of where such compute should go; \mile{} provides one (verification at milestone boundaries) and supports it with scaling curves. Conformal prediction~\cite{conformal} gives distribution-free uncertainty guarantees and has seen growing use in ML; to our knowledge, \mile{} is the first to use conformal calibration as an \emph{execution-time acceptance test} for robot policies, turning a heuristic verification threshold into a statistical guarantee.

\section{Preliminaries and Problem Statement}
\label{sec:prelim}

\paragraph{Setting.}
A language-conditioned manipulation task is a POMDP $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{O}, T, \rho, l)$ with instruction $l$. A chunked VLA policy $\pi_\theta$ maps an observation $o_t$ (multi-view RGB, proprioception) and $l$ to an action chunk $a_{t:t+H} = \pi_\theta(o_t, l)$, executed for $H$ steps before re-querying. Long-horizon tasks decompose into $K$ semantically ordered stages; success requires all stages completed in order.

\paragraph{Compounding error.}
Let $\epsilon$ be the per-step distribution-shift error of behavior cloning. Standard analysis~\cite{ross2011} bounds the divergence between $\pi_\theta$ and expert state distributions by $O(H_{\mathrm{task}} \cdot \epsilon)$ (linear in the task horizon), with \emph{success} (a conjunction over all stages) decaying multiplicatively:
\begin{equation}
P(\mathrm{success}) \;\le\; \prod_{k=1}^{K}\big(1 - h_k \epsilon\big) \;\approx\; 1 - \epsilon \textstyle\sum_k h_k .
\label{eq:compounding}
\end{equation}
For long horizons, $h_k \epsilon \ll 1$ fails and success collapses. Section~\ref{sec:theory} shows how verified re-anchoring truncates each $h_k$ to a bounded, verified segment length.

\subsection{Automatic milestone extraction}
\label{sec:extractor}

Given a demonstration trajectory $\tau = (o_0, a_0, \dots, o_T)$, we extract a sparse milestone index set $\mathcal{M}(\tau) = \{t_1, \dots, t_K\}$ from three complementary cues:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Kinematic events} $c^{\mathrm{kin}}_t$: gripper aperture transitions (open$\leftrightarrow$close crossings), joint-velocity turning points (sign changes in the dominant principal component of end-effector velocity).
  \item \textbf{Contact/progress events} $c^{\mathrm{con}}_t$: sharp changes in predicted contact probability (from a small probe trained on proprioception, or force readings where available).
  \item \textbf{Visual-change events} $c^{\mathrm{vis}}_t$: local maxima of frame-to-frame latent distance $\|f(o_{t}) - f(o_{t-1})\|$ in the frozen VLA's visual encoder $f$, restricted to instruction-relevant regions via the VLA's cross-attention map.
\end{enumerate}
Candidate events are pooled with a minimum separation $\Delta$ and ranked by a consensus score
\begin{equation}
s_t = \alpha\, c^{\mathrm{kin}}_t + \beta\, c^{\mathrm{con}}_t + \gamma\, c^{\mathrm{vis}}_t ;
\end{equation}
the top-$K$ indices (with $K$ estimated per task family from demonstrations) define milestones. Each milestone $m_k$ is represented as a latent $\z_k = f(o_{t_k})$ plus its stage descriptor $\ell_k$ (from the instruction template, e.g., ``\emph{after picking X}''). No human sub-task annotation is required; when task intermediate-step signals are available (as in RoboDojo's sub-step scoring), they are used only as weak supervision to calibrate thresholds $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \Delta)$.

\subsection{Milestone-conditioned world model and progress value}
\label{sec:wm}

The world model $p_\phi$ predicts future feature-space states conditioned on the current observation embedding and a candidate action chunk:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\z}_{t+k} \sim p_\phi\big(\cdot \mid \z_t,\, a_{t:t+k},\, \ell_{k+1}\big).
\end{equation}
We implement $p_\phi$ as a \TODO{4-layer, $d{=}1024$} Transformer with per-token motion conditioning~\cite{ahead}, trained with masked latent prediction on demonstration data plus \TODO{Open X-Embodiment subset}. Following GigaBrain~\cite{gigabrain}, $p_\phi$ shares a backbone with a \textbf{progress value head} $v_\psi(\z, \ell_K)$ predicting normalized steps-to-completion; joint training of prediction and value is essential (ablation in \S\ref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{Verified execution with safe-anchor rollback}
\label{sec:controller}

At each decision point, the controller operates as follows (Algorithm~\ref{alg:mile}):

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\mile{} execution controller}
\label{alg:mile}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Input:} frozen VLA $\pi_\theta$; world model $p_\phi$; value head $v_\psi$; milestone sequence $\{(\z_k, \ell_k)\}_{k=1}^{K}$; thresholds $r, \tau_J$; patience $n$
\State $\mathcal{V} \gets \emptyset$ \Comment{verified-milestone stack}
\For{each decision point $t$}
  \State $m_k \gets \mathrm{top}(\mathcal{V})$;\quad target $\z_{k+1}$
  \State $\{a^{(c)}_{t:t+H}\}_{c=1}^{C} \sim \pi_\theta(o_t, l)$ \Comment{candidate chunks}
  \For{$c = 1 \dots C$}
    \State $\hat{\z}^{(c)}_{t+H} \sim p_\phi(\cdot \mid \z_t, a^{(c)}_{t:t+H}, \ell_{k+1})$ \Comment{imagination}
    \State $J(c) \gets \cos\big(\hat{\z}^{(c)}_{t+H}, \z_{k+1}\big) + \lambda\,[v_\psi(\z_t) - v_\psi(\hat{\z}^{(c)}_{t+H})] - \eta\, u_\phi(\hat{\z}^{(c)}_{t+H})$
  \EndFor
  \State $c^\ast \gets \argmax_c J(c)$
  \If{$J(c^\ast) > \tau_J$}
    \State execute $a^{(c^\ast)}_{t:t+H}$
  \Else
    \State re-sample or retreat to $\mathrm{top}(\mathcal{V})$ \Comment{no verified candidate}
  \EndIf
  \If{realized $\z_{t+H}$ within radius $r$ of $\z_{k+1}$}
    \State push $m_{k+1}$ onto $\mathcal{V}$ \Comment{milestone verified}
  \ElsIf{deviation $> r$ for $n$ consecutive decisions}
    \State retreat to state of $\mathrm{top}(\mathcal{V})$; clear unverified suffix \Comment{rollback}
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Milestone tracking.} Maintain a stack $\mathcal{V}$ of \emph{verified} milestones. The next target is $\z_{k+1}$, where $m_k = \mathrm{top}(\mathcal{V})$.
  \item \textbf{Candidate generation.} Sample $C$ candidate chunks from the (stochastic) base policy.
  \item \textbf{Imagination.} Roll out $p_\phi$ for each candidate to obtain $\hat{\z}^{(c)}_{t+H}$.
  \item \textbf{Verification scoring.} Rank candidates by
  \begin{equation}
  J(c) = \underbrace{\cos\big(\hat{\z}^{(c)}_{t+H},\, \z_{k+1}\big)}_{\text{milestone alignment}} + \lambda \underbrace{\big[v_\psi(\z_t) - v_\psi(\hat{\z}^{(c)}_{t+H})\big]}_{\text{progress}} - \eta \underbrace{u_\phi\big(\hat{\z}^{(c)}_{t+H}\big)}_{\text{uncertainty}},
  \label{eq:score}
  \end{equation}
  where $u_\phi$ is an ensemble/disagreement-based uncertainty estimate. Execute $\argmax_c J(c)$ only if $J(c^\ast) > \tau_J$ (threshold calibrated on validation rollouts).
  \item \textbf{Advance or rollback.} After execution, if the realized latent is within radius $r$ of a verified milestone, push it onto $\mathcal{V}$. If the realized latent deviates from the predicted next milestone beyond radius $r$ for $n$ consecutive decisions, \textbf{retreat}: reset (physically or via the best re-approach policy) to the state of $\mathrm{top}(\mathcal{V})$, clear the unverified suffix, and re-plan from that anchor.
\end{enumerate}

The full controller requires no retraining of $\pi_\theta$: only $p_\phi$, $v_\psi$, and the extractor thresholds are learned (\TODO{X}M parameters total).

\subsection{Conformal calibration of the acceptance test}
\label{sec:conformal}

The scoring rule (Eq.~\ref{eq:score}) needs a threshold $\tau_J$, and milestone verification needs a radius $r$. Rather than tuning both heuristically, we calibrate the acceptance test with \textbf{split-conformal prediction}, which yields a distribution-free, finite-sample guarantee.

Fix the verification radius $r$ (chosen once from kinematic task scale, \TODO{X}). On a held-out set of $n$ calibration rollouts from the \emph{base policy} (no \mile{} components tuned on them), record for every executed chunk $i$ the pair $(J_i, d_i)$, where $J_i$ is the verification score of the executed candidate and $d_i = \|\z^{\mathrm{real}}_i - \z_{k+1}\|$ is the realized deviation from the target milestone. We choose the acceptance threshold as
\begin{equation}
\hat{\tau} \;=\; \min\Big\{\, \tau \;:\; \widehat{Q}_{1-\alpha}\big(\{ d_i : J_i \ge \tau \}\big) \;\le\; r \,\Big\},
\label{eq:conformal}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{Q}_{1-\alpha}$ denotes the empirical $\lceil (1-\alpha)(n_\tau + 1) \rceil / n_\tau$ quantile over the $n_\tau$ calibration points passing $\tau$. By exchangeability of calibration and test points, a standard argument (Appendix~\ref{app:proof}) gives:

\begin{proposition}[Marginal coverage of accepted segments]
\label{prop:coverage}
For a new decision point drawn from the same distribution, the executed chunk passes the acceptance test and terminates within radius $r$ of its target milestone with probability at least $1 - \alpha - \frac{1}{n+1}$.
\end{proposition}

The guarantee is \emph{marginal} over accepted decisions (not conditional on the state), requires no assumption about the world model's accuracy, and degrades gracefully: a worse world model simply yields a stricter $\hat{\tau}$ (more rejections, more rollbacks), not more false accepts. We use $\alpha = \TODO{0.05}$ throughout and verify coverage empirically (\S\ref{sec:coverage}).

\subsection{Theory: verified re-anchoring truncates error compounding}
\label{sec:theory}

\begin{proposition}[Success bound with conformal re-anchoring]
\label{prop:main}
Let a task be segmented into $K$ stages by milestones. Suppose (i) each accepted segment terminates within radius $r$ of its target milestone with probability at least $1-\alpha$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:coverage}), and (ii) rollback restores a state within the $r$-neighborhood of the last verified milestone with probability at least $1-\delta$, with at most $M$ retreat attempts per stage. Then
\begin{equation}
P(\mathrm{success}) \;\ge\; 1 - K\big(\alpha + M\delta\big),
\end{equation}
whereas the open-loop base policy satisfies $P(\mathrm{success}) \le \prod_{k=1}^{K}(1 - h_k \epsilon)$ with per-stage horizon $h_k$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:compounding}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Union bound over $K$ stages: a task fails only if some stage is accepted erroneously (probability $\le \alpha$ each) or its $M$ retreats all fail (probability $\le \delta^M$, absorbed into $M\delta$). Within an accepted segment, deviation is bounded by $r$ by definition, so errors cannot propagate across stages. The open-loop policy has $r = \infty$ and no acceptance test, so per-step errors accumulate within each full stage ($h_k \epsilon$) and multiply across stages. Full proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The contrast is structural: open-loop failure probability grows with the \emph{total} horizon because error budgets are unchecked inside stages; \mile{}'s failure probability is controlled by two \emph{user-chosen} constants $(\alpha, \delta)$, independent of how long each stage is. The parameters $r$ and $M$ trade guarantee strength against rollback frequency and latency; \S\ref{sec:ablation} ablates both.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Self-improvement from hindsight recovery data}
\label{sec:selfimprove}

Rollback events are usually treated as wasted computation. \mile{} instead converts them into training signal. Whenever a retreat to anchor $m_k$ is followed by successfully reaching $m_{k+1}$, the trajectory segment (anchor state $\rightarrow$ milestone) is stored in a \emph{recovery buffer} with a hindsight milestone label---a free demonstration of \emph{how to recover from the boundary of failure}. Every $R$ deployment rounds (\TODO{$R{=}500$ episodes}), the buffer is distilled by a lightweight update: the value head $v_\psi$ is fine-tuned on buffer states (improving progress estimation exactly where the base policy struggles), and optionally a LoRA adapter on the base's action expert is trained on recovery segments. No rewards, human labels, or new task demonstrations are required. \S\ref{sec:rounds} shows success improves monotonically over \TODO{3} rounds.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

(i) The world model inherits the base's perception; if $f$ fails to distinguish two states (strong perceptual aliasing), verification cannot rescue execution (\S\ref{sec:taxonomy}). (ii) Rollback assumes stages are physically reversible or re-approachable; irreversible operations (cutting, pouring) need one-way milestone flags---we currently exclude \TODO{X} such tasks. (iii) Verification latency, though small (\TODO{X}ms), may matter in highly dynamic scenes; adaptive candidate budgets are future work. (iv) Absolute success rates on RoboDojo remain far below human level; \mile{} narrows but does not close the gap.

% ======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \mile{}, a model-based wrapper that turns open-loop VLA policies into closed-loop long-horizon agents by grounding imagination in automatically discovered physical milestones, verifying action chunks before commitment, and retreating to safe anchors on deviation. Both theory (additive vs.\ multiplicative error bounds) and experiments (RoboDojo long-horizon/memory gains on two bases) support the central claim: \emph{long-horizon failure is largely a systems problem, and closing the loop at the right granularity is a cheap and general way to fix it.}
